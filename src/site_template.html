2 Structure and formulation

Engineering problem

The bracket connects an engine to the chassis. It must carry inertial loads without yielding, be stiff enough that the engine mount point barely moves, keep its first natural frequency above the engine's excitation band, and must not buckle. Mass is the objective. The design domain, interfaces and loads are team modelling assumptions (table below), chosen so that the problem is representative but simple to reproduce.

Coordinates. \(x\) along the bracket length (0–200 mm), \(y\) across (0–120 mm), \(z\) vertical, up (0–160 mm). The base is \(z=0\); the engine sits on the top face \(z=160\) mm.

Design variables

One density variable \(x_e \in [10^{-3}, 1]\) per non-passive voxel: @@n_design@@ variables on the @@h_mm@@ mm mesh (@@ne@@ voxels minus the passive pads and interface). The lower bound keeps the relaxed stress \(\bar\rho^{q}\) differentiable; voxels with \(\bar\rho\to 0\) are void. Physical densities \(\bar\rho\) follow from the filter and projection in section 3.

Optimization problem

\[ \min_{\mathbf x}\; m(\mathbf x)=\rho_0 V_e \sum_e \bar\rho_e(\mathbf x) \quad\text{s.t.}\quad g_i(\mathbf x)\le 0,\; i=1,\dots,9,\qquad 10^{-3}\le x_e\le 1 , \]

with the scaled objective \(f = m/m_{solid}\) and all constraints dimensionless, feasible when \(\le 0\):

\[ \begin{aligned} g_{\sigma,k} &= \frac{1}{\sigma_{allow}}\Big(\sum_{e}\sum_{\text{GP}}\big(\bar\rho_e^{\,q}\,\sigma_{vm}\big)^{P}\Big)^{1/P}-1, & k&=1,2,3, & \sigma_{allow}&=276/1.5=@@sig_allow@@\ \text{MPa}\\ g_{d,k} &= \frac{\lVert\bar{\mathbf u}_k\rVert}{d_{allow}}-1, \quad \bar{\mathbf u}_k=\textstyle\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{if}} w_n\mathbf u_{n,k}, & k&=1,2,3, & d_{allow}&=0.5\ \text{mm}\\ g_{f} &= 1-\mathrm{KS}_{\min}\big(\lambda_1/\lambda_{req},\dots,\lambda_6/\lambda_{req}\big), & \lambda_{req}&=(2\pi\cdot 100)^2=@@lam_req@@\ \text{s}^{-2} \\ g_{b,k} &= \mathrm{KS}_{\max}\big(\alpha_{req}\mu_{1,k},\dots,\alpha_{req}\mu_{4,k}\big)-1, \quad \mu=1/\alpha, & k&=2,3, & \alpha_{req}&=3 \end{aligned} \]

\(\bar{\mathbf u}_k\) is the area-weighted mean displacement of the engine-interface nodes (the translation of the mount point); its norm covers every direction. \(\mathrm{KS}_{\max}(v)=\max v+\tfrac1s\ln\sum e^{s(v_i-\max v)}\ge\max v\) and \(\mathrm{KS}_{\min}(v)=-\mathrm{KS}_{\max}(-v)\le\min v\) with \(s=50\) [9]; both are conservative and smooth when eigenvalues are repeated or cross [22]. The p-norm (\(P=8\)) over all Gauss points of all voxels is an upper bound on the true maximum, so \(g_\sigma\le 0\) guarantees the relaxed maximum stress is below the allowable. The buckling factor of LC1 needs no constraint: its prestress is the mean of LC2 and LC3, and for the Rayleigh quotient \(\mu_1=\max_\psi \psi^T(-K_G^{(1)})\psi/\psi^TK\psi \le \max(\mu_2,\mu_3)\), so \(\alpha_{LC1}\ge\min(\alpha_{LC2},\alpha_{LC3})\).

All limits (184 MPa, 0.5 mm, 100 Hz, α ≥ 3, 3 g, 400 N·m) come from the project brief and are team design assumptions, not code requirements. The safety factor 1.5 on yield matches the common aerospace ultimate factor [14 CFR 25.303] but is applied here to typical yield as a team choice.

3 Analysis model

Governing equations

\[ \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma+\mathbf b=\mathbf 0 \text{ in }\Omega,\quad \boldsymbol\sigma=\mathbf C:\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\mathbf u),\quad \boldsymbol\varepsilon=\tfrac12(\nabla\mathbf u+\nabla\mathbf u^T),\quad \mathbf u=\mathbf 0 \text{ on }\Gamma_{pads},\quad \boldsymbol\sigma\mathbf n=\mathbf t_k \text{ on }\Gamma_{if}. \]

Weak form: find \(\mathbf u\) with \(\int_\Omega \boldsymbol\varepsilon(\mathbf v):\mathbf C:\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\mathbf u)\,d\Omega =\int_{\Gamma_{if}}\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf t_k\,d\Gamma\) for all admissible \(\mathbf v\), discretized as \(\mathbf K\mathbf u_k=\mathbf F_k\). Free vibration: \(\mathbf K\boldsymbol\phi=\lambda\mathbf M\boldsymbol\phi,\ \lambda=\omega^2\), with \(\boldsymbol\phi^T\mathbf M\boldsymbol\phi=1\). Linear buckling about the static state of load case \(k\): \((\mathbf K+\alpha\mathbf K_G(\mathbf u_k))\boldsymbol\psi=\mathbf 0\), solved as the symmetric-definite problem \((-\mathbf K_G)\boldsymbol\psi=\mu\mathbf K\boldsymbol\psi\), \(\mu=1/\alpha\), largest \(\mu\) first, \(\boldsymbol\psi^T\mathbf K\boldsymbol\psi=1\) [18, 19]. Stress is tension-positive, so compression makes \(-\mathbf K_G\) positive and \(\alpha>0\).

Element

8-node trilinear hexahedra, 2×2×2 Gauss quadrature, with Wilson's 9 incompatible bubble modes and Taylor's centroid-Jacobian correction [16, 17], condensed out at element level (\(\mathbf K_e=\mathbf K_{uu}-\mathbf K_{u\alpha}\mathbf K_{\alpha\alpha}^{-1}\mathbf K_{\alpha u}\)). The plain fully integrated hex8 locks in bending: 33 % too stiff on the V2 cantilever with two elements through the depth. Stresses are recovered at the Gauss points with the condensed bubble modes (\(\boldsymbol\sigma_g=\mathbf D(\mathbf B_u+\mathbf B_\alpha\mathbf A)\mathbf u_e\)). Mass is consistent (bubbles carry none). The geometric stiffness uses the Gauss-point stresses: \(\mathbf K_{G,e}=\sum_g \mathbf G_g^T\mathbf S(\boldsymbol\sigma_g)\mathbf G_g\,\det J_g\). Global matrices are SciPy sparse; one sparse LU of \(\mathbf K\) serves all load cases, every adjoint solve and the shift-invert eigen-solves (ARPACK).

SIMP model, filter and projection

\[ \tilde\rho=\mathbf H\mathbf y,\quad H_{ej}\propto\max(0,r-\lVert\mathbf c_e-\mathbf c_j\rVert),\ r=30\ \text{mm};\qquad \bar\rho=\frac{\tanh\beta\eta+\tanh\beta(\tilde\rho-\eta)}{\tanh\beta\eta+\tanh\beta(1-\eta)},\ \eta=0.5 \] \[ E_K=E_0\big(\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)\bar\rho^{\,p}\big),\ \varepsilon=10^{-9};\qquad E_G=E_0\,\bar\rho^{\,p};\qquad m_{modal}=\bar\rho^{\,p}\frac{1+c}{\bar\rho^{\,p-1}+c},\ c=0.1^{\,p-1};\qquad \boldsymbol\sigma=\bar\rho^{\,q}\,\mathbf D_0\mathbf B\mathbf u,\ q=\tfrac12 \]

\(\mathbf y\) equals \(\mathbf x\) on design voxels and 1 on passive voxels. The density filter [3, 4] fixes a 30 mm minimum length scale independent of the mesh and removes checkerboards; the projection [5] drives the field to 0/1 as \(\beta\) grows. SIMP stiffness [1, 2] penalizes intermediate densities. The stress used in \(\mathbf K_G\) has no floor, so void voxels carry no prestress and create no spurious buckling modes [6, 7]. The modal mass equals the physical mass at \(\bar\rho=0\) and 1 but falls like \(\bar\rho^{\,p}\) near zero (a smooth variant of Pedersen's correction [12, 13]), so soft voids cannot create spurious low-frequency local modes; the objective always uses the physical mass \(\bar\rho\). Stress relaxation with \(q<p\) [8, 10, 11] removes the singular optimum of stress-constrained topology problems.

5 Sensitivities

Course method (ii): JAX differentiates only element-level functions (the Gauss-point stress measure and the element quadratic form \(\boldsymbol\psi_e^T\mathbf K_{G,e}(\mathbf u_e)\boldsymbol\psi_e\)); global adjoints are assembled by hand with SciPy sparse and solved with the existing factorization of \(\mathbf K\). Method (i), a dense JAX pipeline differentiated end to end, is used only as an independent check. No Jacobian-free or "phantom" gradient is used anywhere.

Static responses

\[ \mathbf K\boldsymbol\lambda=-\Big(\frac{\partial g}{\partial\mathbf u}\Big)^T,\qquad \frac{dg}{d\bar\rho_e}=\frac{\partial g}{\partial\bar\rho_e}+\boldsymbol\lambda_e^T\frac{\partial\mathbf K_e}{\partial\bar\rho_e}\mathbf u_e =\frac{\partial g}{\partial\bar\rho_e}+E_K'(\bar\rho_e)\,\boldsymbol\lambda_e^T\mathbf K_e^{(1)}\mathbf u_e \]

(loads do not depend on the design). Six adjoint right-hand sides (3 stress norms, 3 displacements) are solved together.

Frequencies

\[ \frac{d\lambda_i}{d\bar\rho_e}=\boldsymbol\phi_{i,e}^T\Big(\frac{\partial\mathbf K_e}{\partial\bar\rho_e}-\lambda_i\frac{\partial\mathbf M_e}{\partial\bar\rho_e}\Big)\boldsymbol\phi_{i,e}, \qquad \frac{dg_f}{d\bar\rho_e}=-\sum_i w_i\frac{1}{\lambda_{req}}\frac{d\lambda_i}{d\bar\rho_e} \]

with mass-normalized modes and KS weights \(w_i\). For a repeated eigenvalue the individual derivatives are not unique, but the KS weights of a degenerate cluster are equal, so the weighted sum equals the derivative of the trace over the cluster, which is basis independent [22]. V3.p and V4.p confirm the repeated pairs are resolved.

Buckling, including the dependence of KG on the static state

\[ \frac{d\mu_i}{d\mathbf x}=-\boldsymbol\psi_i^T\frac{\partial\mathbf K_G}{\partial\mathbf x}\Big|_{\mathbf u}\boldsymbol\psi_i-\mu_i\boldsymbol\psi_i^T\frac{\partial\mathbf K}{\partial\mathbf x}\boldsymbol\psi_i -\mathbf c_i^T\frac{d\mathbf u}{d\mathbf x},\qquad \mathbf c_i=\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf u}\big(\boldsymbol\psi_i^T\mathbf K_G(\mathbf u)\boldsymbol\psi_i\big) \]

The last term is the prestress-through-state contribution. With \(\mathbf K\mathbf u=\mathbf F\) and one adjoint solve per aggregate, \(\mathbf K\boldsymbol\lambda_b=\alpha_{req}\sum_i w_i\mathbf c_i\), it becomes \(+\boldsymbol\lambda_b^T(\partial\mathbf K/\partial\mathbf x)\mathbf u\). \(\mathbf c_{i,e}\) comes from JAX (element level). Treating the prestress as constant would drop this term; the method (i) check below differentiates through the static solve and agrees to round-off.

Filter and projection

\[ \frac{dg}{dx_j}=\sum_e \frac{dg}{d\bar\rho_e}\,\frac{d\bar\rho_e}{d\tilde\rho_e}\,H_{ej}\quad (\text{passive voxels: } d\bar\rho_e/d\tilde\rho_e=0). \]

6 Optimization

Algorithm. Method of Moving Asymptotes [14, 15], implemented in src/mma.py (no external optimizer), with elastic variables (penalty \(c=1000\)) that keep every subproblem feasible, and its dual solved with L-BFGS-B. SLSQP was rejected because it keeps a dense \(n\times n\) quasi-Newton matrix and solves a dense QP per iteration (\(n\) = @@n_design@@ here). MMA is checked in the test suite on \(\min\sum x\) s.t. \(\sum 1/x\le n\) (exact optimum \(x=1\), multiplier 1).

Scaling. Variables are in [0, 1]; the objective is mass / solid mass; every constraint is a ratio to its limit minus one.

Continuation. Penalization and projection are raised in stages; the asymptotes are reset at each stage. The schedule starts at p = 2: in development, p = 1 let MMA remove nearly all material in a few steps (gray designs are artificially efficient at p = 1) and it could not recover. The last stage uses a smaller move limit. A stage ends when max |Δx| < 0.002 with every constraint satisfied, or at its iteration limit. The reported design is the last iterate if it is feasible, otherwise the lowest-mass feasible iterate of the last stage. A: @@chosen_A@@; B: @@chosen_B@@.

KKT conditions and shadow prices

KKT multipliers are estimated independently of MMA: for the near-active constraints (\(g>-0.01\)), non-negative least squares on \(\nabla f+\sum_i\lambda_i\nabla g_i=\mathbf 0\) over the variables strictly between their bounds; the remaining gradient components are the bound multipliers and must have the correct sign on the bounds where they act. Interpretation. \(\lambda_i\) is the decrease of \(f=m/m_{solid}\) per unit relaxation of \(g_i\). Tightening the frequency requirement by 1 % raises \(\lambda_{req}\) by about 2 % and \(g_f\) by about 0.02, so the mass cost is \(\approx\lambda_f\cdot0.02\cdot m_{solid}\); the last column applies this to each near-active constraint. A zero multiplier on buckling means the stability requirement costs nothing at this optimum. 7 Nonlinear component: linear buckling (N1)

Buckling is linearized about the computed static state of LC2 and LC3 (section 3), and its sensitivity includes the state dependence of \(\mathbf K_G\) (section 5). Designs with (B) and without (A) the constraint \(\alpha_{cr}\ge3\):

@@fig_buckling@@

Main problem. Without the constraint, the optimum already has \(\alpha_{cr}\) = @@alpha_A@@; with it, @@alpha_B@@. The constraint is inactive with a zero multiplier, and the masses differ by @@dm_AB@@ %, which is within the scatter of the optimizer path. The frequency requirement with a 120 kg engine forces a lateral stiffness that only stocky members provide, and stocky members do not buckle at 3× the service load.

Supplementary study (20 mm mesh). To see when stability matters, the frequency constraint is removed (as for a soft-mounted engine), so stress and displacement size the bracket, and the buckling requirement is then raised. This is a separate study, not the project's design case.

@@nl_table@@

Without frequency or buckling constraints the design is @@mass_N@@ kg with \(\alpha_{cr}\) = @@alpha_N@@: buckling is still not critical at \(\alpha_{req}=3\). Requirements above that value become active (see the near-active column), and the optimizer then shapes the compression members to meet them. The mass does not rise monotonically with the requirement: some constrained runs end lighter than the unconstrained one. These are different local optima of a nonconvex problem started from the same design, so differences of a few percent between these runs are within the optimizer's path scatter and should not be read as the cost of stability.

9 Bonus: topology optimization

The design is a material distribution in which material may vanish: SIMP with penalization continuation, a 30 mm density filter (minimum length scale, checkerboard suppression), Heaviside projection to a crisp 0/1 field, stress relaxation against the singular optimum, void-safe mass and prestress interpolations, and a comparison with the unoptimized solid domain and between two mesh sizes. Whether this counts toward the topology bonus, given that the base problem is itself topology optimization, is for the instructor to decide.

10 Limitations and AI-use statement

Limitations

  • Linear elasticity and linear (eigenvalue) buckling only: no plasticity, no geometric nonlinearity, no imperfection sensitivity. Real buckling capacity of thin members can be well below the linear estimate; here the margin is large.
  • Supports are bonded pads; real bolts, contact, preload and slip are not modelled. Loads are uniform/linear tractions on an idealized patch.
  • Stress at the clamped pad edges and the patch corners is singular in linear elasticity (section 4), so the maximum stress is mesh dependent. The relaxed, aggregated stress is an engineering measure, not a certification value.
  • Voxel resolution @@h_mm@@ mm: surfaces are stair-stepped, and the first frequency of the optimized design drops to @@ref5_f1@@ Hz on a 5 mm mesh (section 8). The design is feasible on its analysis mesh; a production design needs a frequency margin or a finer mesh.
  • Intermediate densities remain at boundaries (see grayness in the results table); the thresholded check quantifies the effect.
  • No fatigue (engine vibration is cyclic), no thermal loads, no manufacturing validation. 276 MPa is a typical yield strength; a design-allowable (MMPDS) value is lower.
  • The engine is a lumped translational mass; its rotary inertia, which would lower rocking modes, is neglected.
  • No comparison with commercial FE software was carried out; verification relies on the analytical ladder and the independent method (i) check.
  • MMA without globally convergent safeguards (GCMMA); topology optimization has many local optima.

AI-use statement

The repository, including the solver, sensitivities, optimizer, tests, figures, this page and the 3D viewer, was written by an AI coding agent (Anthropic's Claude, in Claude Code) working from the team's specification. Correctness rests on checks the code runs itself: the verification ladder against closed-form theory, central-difference and Taylor tests of every gradient, and an independent dense automatic-differentiation pipeline. Bugs caught by these checks during development:

  • Geometric stiffness scaled twice by E0. The bracket's \(\mathbf K_G\) map already contained \(E_0\) and the interpolation multiplied it again, giving \(\alpha_{cr}\approx 0\). The finite-difference check passed (the error was self-consistent); comparing with an independent element-by-element assembly (now check V5.kg) exposed it.
  • Engine mass applied to the wrong DOFs in the method (i) check. The dense check sliced an already-reduced matrix a second time; the frequency value disagreed with the sparse solver, and scipy.linalg.eigh on the sparse model's matrices showed the sparse solver was right.
  • YAML parsed 69.0e9 as a string (YAML 1.1 needs an exponent sign); the first test run failed and the config now uses 69.0e+9.
  • Badly scaled frequency constraint. The first form \(\lambda_{req}/\lambda_1-1\) exploded as the design thinned and MMA oscillated at +20; it was replaced by the bounded, nearly linear \(1-\mathrm{KS}_{\min}(\lambda_i/\lambda_{req})\) (same feasible set).
